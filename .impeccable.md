# Impeccable Design Context — Call Me Yours (CMY)

## Design Context

### Users
Young adults ages 18–35 in Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, and South Africa. Predominantly Android users running MiniPay as their primary financial app. They use CMY while commuting, outdoors, in cafes — bright environments with variable connectivity. They are digitally fluent but skeptical of foreign tech products that don't understand their context. They've been burned by fake profiles and dating app bots before. They use cUSD for everyday transactions.

**Job to be done:** Find real connections with real people. Skip the bots, skip the catfish, skip the ghosting economy.

**Emotional starting point:** Cautious optimism. They want to trust this space but they need to be shown it's different before they'll relax into it.

### Brand Personality
**3 words: warm, real, hopeful**

Not warm like a cozy tech startup — warm like a message from someone you already trust. Not real like "authentic brand voice" — real like face-to-face accountability. Not hopeful like naive optimism — hopeful like someone who's been hurt before but still believes.

CMY is the friend who vouches for the person you're about to meet.

### Emotional Arc
**Safety first, then playful curiosity.** The first impression must communicate trust — this is a verified space with real accountability. Once a user feels safe, the interface should invite them to be curious, warm, even playful. Delight emerges through use, not decoration.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Light mode primary.** Users are outdoors and commuting; dark mode is secondary at best. The palette should feel warm and human — romantic without being saccharine, grounded without being corporate.

**Palette:** Celo green (`#07955F`) exists as a subtle on-chain action accent only — it signals a blockchain moment (a payment, a confirmation), not the brand. CMY's own palette should lean warm: think terracotta, warm amber, deep aubergine, or soft clay tones. Something that feels like the colors in an Accra market or a Lagos evening — not Silicon Valley teal.

**Typography:**
- **Display/Headings:** Rozha One — editorial weight, romantic boldness, warmth without coldness
- **Body/UI:** Epilogue — humanist sans, variable font, excellent Android legibility

**Anti-references (what CMY must never look like):**
- Tinder's swipe casino aesthetic — gamified, disposable, dopamine-trap
- Neon crypto dark mode — blockchain-green glows on black
- Hinge's cold clinical profile cards — grey, sanitized, emotionally flat
- Generic fintech dashboards — blue gradients, hero metrics, trust badges

**References (what captures the right energy):**
- WhatsApp's familiarity — feels like a space you already know
- Duolingo's warmth — human without being childish
- Local-first apps that feel like they were made for the person using them, not for an investor demo

### Design Principles

1. **Trust is the first emotion.** Every surface must communicate safety and accountability before anything else. No ambiguity about what CMY is and why it's different.

2. **Warmth over polish.** Slightly rough is fine if it feels human. Overly polished feels fake and foreign. CMY is not trying to look like a Y Combinator pitch deck.

3. **Designed for the actual context.** Bright screens, Android mid-range hardware, potentially slow connections. High contrast, fast loading, no heavy blur effects or GPU-intensive visuals.

4. **Connection over transaction.** cUSD payments exist in the product but must never feel transactional. Payment moments should feel like a meaningful gesture, not a fee.

5. **Playful curiosity emerges through interaction.** Animation and delight should be discovered, not imposed. The interface starts calm and opens up as users engage.
